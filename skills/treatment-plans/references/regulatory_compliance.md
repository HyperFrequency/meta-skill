# Regulatory Compliance

De-identification and the regulatory constraints that shape treatment-plan
documentation. Verify current requirements — regulations change and vary by state.

## HIPAA and de-identification (do this first)

Protected Health Information (PHI) is any health/care/payment information linkable to
an individual. Before a plan leaves the treating team, de-identify by the **Safe
Harbor method** — remove all 18 identifiers:

1. Names
2. Geographic units smaller than a state (street, city, county, ZIP if area <20,000)
3. Dates (birth, admission, discharge, death) except year
4. Telephone numbers
5. Fax numbers
6. Email addresses
7. Social Security numbers
8. Medical record numbers
9. Health-plan beneficiary numbers
10. Account numbers
11. Certificate/license numbers
12. Vehicle identifiers/serial numbers (incl. license plate)
13. Device identifiers/serial numbers
14. Web URLs
15. IP addresses
16. Biometric identifiers (fingerprints, voiceprints)
17. Full-face photographs and comparable images
18. Any other unique identifying number, characteristic, or code

**Practical substitutions:** name → "Patient" or a code (PT-001); exact age → age
range ("55-60 years"); dates → relative ("3 months ago") or month/year; location →
state only; remove MRN/account/SSN.

> Before: "John Smith, DOB 3/15/1965, MRN 123456, 123 Main St, Anytown CA 12345,
> seen 1/15/2025." After: "Patient, age 55-60, seen [Month]/2025, California."

**Permitted without authorization:** treatment, payment, healthcare operations
(minimum-necessary standard applies — but *not* to treatment disclosures).
**Requires authorization:** marketing, most research (absent IRB waiver), disclosure
to non-covered entities, psychotherapy notes. Patients have rights to access,
amendment, and an accounting of disclosures. Breaches: notify individuals within 60
days; notify HHS/media if ≥500 affected. Penalties run from civil ($100-$50,000 per
violation) to criminal (up to $250,000 and 10 years for knowing misuse).

## 42 CFR Part 2 (substance-use records)

More restrictive than HIPAA for federally assisted SUD-treatment records. Generally
requires **written, specific, time-limited patient consent** even for treatment/
payment/operations, and prohibits re-disclosure by recipients. Consent-free
exceptions: medical emergency, court order (not a subpoena alone), suspected child
abuse/neglect, on-premises crime. In a plan, annotate: "Substance-use information
subject to 42 CFR Part 2 confidentiality protections."

## 21 CFR Part 11 (FDA electronic records)

Applies to clinical trials and FDA-regulated research — requires system validation,
audit trails, and electronic signatures. If the plan is part of a trial protocol it
must meet these plus IRB approval (21 CFR Part 56) and informed consent (21 CFR Part
50). Routine non-research care is governed by the HIPAA Security Rule instead.

## CMS (Medicare/Medicaid)

Conditions of Participation require physician orders, comprehensive care plans,
periodic reassessment/revision, interdisciplinary and patient/family involvement.
Document to support **medical necessity** and E/M coding: diagnosis (ICD-10),
services (CPT), rationale, patient response, ongoing-care plan. E/M complexity scales
from low (stable chronic) to high (severe conditions, high-risk treatments, poor
response).

## Quality-measure reporting

- **HEDIS** (health-plan quality): e.g., diabetes — HbA1c testing, annual dilated eye
  exam, urine albumin-to-creatinine, BP <140/90; cardiovascular — statin with
  diabetes/ASCVD, ACE/ARB with diabetes+HTN, beta-blocker post-MI/HFrEF; preventive —
  flu vaccine, colorectal/breast/cervical screening.
- **MIPS** (Medicare Part B): quality, improvement activities, promoting
  interoperability, cost. **ACOs:** 33+ measures across experience, coordination,
  prevention, at-risk populations. Structured plan data enables measure extraction.

## Opioid prescribing

**CDC guidelines:** prefer non-opioid therapy for chronic pain; if opioids, lowest
effective dose and shortest duration; assess risk (ORT, SOAPP) before starting;
prescribe naloxone for elevated overdose risk; UDS before and during; check PDMP
before prescribing; avoid concurrent benzodiazepines; reassess risk/benefit at each
dose increase (especially approaching ≥50 MME/day). Document indication, informed
consent, treatment agreement, monitoring plan (UDS/PDMP frequency), functional goals,
and a tapering/exit strategy. **State rules vary** — MME caps, acute-pain supply
limits, mandatory PDMP checks, naloxone co-prescription. **PDMP:** state databases;
note that it was checked and the findings.

## State medical-board and standard-of-care

Prescribe within scope of practice (physician, NP, PA authority varies by state; DEA
registration for controlled substances). Document rationale for any deviation from
standard of care. Record informed consent for procedures, surgeries, and high-risk
medications (nature of condition and treatment, risks/benefits, alternatives, outcome
if untreated, questions answered, capacity, voluntary consent). Retain records per
state law (typically 7-10 years; longer for minors).

## Accreditation

- **Joint Commission:** individualized, patient-and-family-centered care planning;
  care coordination across transitions; medication reconciliation and high-risk-med
  monitoring (anticoagulants, opioids, insulin); National Patient Safety Goals.
- **CARF** (rehab/behavioral health): comprehensive assessment drives the plan;
  individualized, measurable, time-specific, person-centered goals; regular team
  review; transition/discharge planning; outcomes measurement.

## Mental-health-specific law

- **Duty to warn/protect (Tarasoff, varies by state):** credible threat to an
  identifiable person may require warning the victim, notifying police, and
  protective steps — document the threat assessment and actions.
- **Involuntary commitment:** typically requires mental illness plus danger to
  self/others or grave disability; emergency hold (24-72 h) then court hearing —
  document dangerousness and least-restrictive efforts.
- **Parity (MHPAEA):** mental-health/SUD benefits must be comparable to
  medical/surgical benefits.

## Billing integrity

Code to the highest specificity (ICD-10) and use correct CPT codes/modifiers; do not
unbundle. Avoid upcoding, duplicate billing, and billing for services not rendered.
Be aware of the Anti-Kickback Statute (no remuneration for federally reimbursed
referrals) and the Stark Law (no physician self-referral for designated health
services).

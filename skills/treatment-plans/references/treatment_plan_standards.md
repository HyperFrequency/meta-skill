# Treatment Plan Standards

What a complete, professional treatment plan must contain, how to lead with a
scannable summary, the documentation quality bar, and when to revise. Use this as
the structural checklist for the plan body.

## Lead with a first-page summary (Foundation-Medicine style)

Precision-medicine reports (e.g., genomic profiling) open with a dense, at-a-glance
panel so a busy clinician gets the actionable content in ~30 seconds. Do the same:
the entire first page is an executive summary, and detail begins on page 2+.

First page contains **only**:

1. **Title + subtitle** — plan type and specific condition/patient descriptor.
2. **Report/patient info box** — de-identified demographics, primary diagnosis with
   ICD-10 code, plan date, framework used.
3. **2-4 key-findings boxes** — Primary Goals (2-3 SMART goals), Core Interventions
   (2-3 items), Critical Decision Points / safety thresholds (if any), and a brief
   Timeline overview.

A strong first page can often stand alone; the rest is supporting detail, not
repetition. Do not repeat first-page content verbatim in later sections.

## Essential components (standard body)

Include, in a logical order, the sections that apply to the case:

- **Patient information (de-identified).** Age range, sex, relevant background,
  active conditions/comorbidities, current meds and allergies, functional baseline.
  Strip all 18 HIPAA identifiers (see `regulatory_compliance.md`).
- **Diagnosis and assessment.** Primary diagnosis + ICD-10; secondary diagnoses;
  severity/staging (e.g., CKD 1-5, GOLD I-IV, NYHA I-IV); functional limitations;
  risk stratification; prognosis.
- **Treatment goals (SMART).** Short-term (weeks-3 mo) and long-term (3-12+ mo),
  distinguished. See `goal_setting.md`.
- **Interventions.** Pharmacological (drug, dose, route, frequency, rationale,
  titration), non-pharmacological (lifestyle, behavioral, education, self-
  monitoring), procedural (procedures, referrals, testing, preventive care). See
  `interventions.md`.
- **Timeline and schedule.** Treatment phases with durations, visit frequency,
  milestone assessments, expected total duration.
- **Monitoring parameters.** Outcomes to track (labs, vitals, symptoms), validated
  scales, frequency, and thresholds for escalation.
- **Expected outcomes.** Primary outcome measures, success criteria, timeline for
  improvement, criteria for modifying therapy, prognosis.
- **Follow-up plan.** Appointments, communication plan, emergency procedures,
  criteria for urgent evaluation, transition/discharge planning.
- **Patient education.** Condition understanding, self-management skills, adherence,
  warning signs, resources. Keep to 3-5 critical bullets.
- **Risk mitigation.** Key adverse effects and management, interactions/
  contraindications, emergency action plans, safety monitoring. Highlight only the
  critical concerns, not exhaustive lists.

## Documentation quality bar

- **Specificity over vagueness.** "Reduce HbA1c from 8.5% to <7% within 3 months"
  not "improve diabetes control." Define uncommon abbreviations on first use.
- **Completeness.** Address the relevant aspects of the condition; give a rationale
  for each treatment choice; document shared decision-making and patient preferences.
- **Accuracy.** Correct dosing/frequency, current evidence, correct ICD-10/CPT codes.
- **Timeliness.** Create at diagnosis/treatment start; update on significant change.
- **Organization.** Consistent headings, logical flow, easy to locate key items;
  dated and authenticated (signature + credentials).

### Common deficiencies → fixes

| Deficiency | Fix |
| --- | --- |
| Vague goal ("improve diabetes") | "Reduce HbA1c 8.5%→<7% in 3 mo via med intensification + lifestyle" |
| Medication with no rationale | "Metformin 1000 mg BID — first-line T2DM, reduces hepatic glucose output" |
| Goal with no timeline | "Short-term (3 mo): HbA1c <7.5%; long-term (6 mo): <7%" |
| "Monitor labs" | "HbA1c q3mo until at goal then q6mo; CMP q6mo for renal function" |
| No patient education documented | Dedicated section: skills taught, warning signs, resources |
| No safety planning | Name the specific risk (e.g., hypoglycemia), monitoring, and patient action |

## Quality indicators (audit targets)

- All required sections present (100%); goals meet SMART criteria (≥90%);
  interventions have rationales (≥95%); monitoring includes frequency (≥95%);
  patient education documented (100%).
- Evidence-based interventions (≥90%); guideline-concordant (≥85%); low-value care
  avoided (100%).
- Patient preferences documented (≥90%); shared decision-making noted (≥85%);
  health literacy addressed (≥90%).
- Safety: risk mitigation, medication safety, emergency procedures, warning signs
  (100% each).

## Medical necessity and legal defensibility

Documentation should demonstrate the intervention fits the diagnosis, that evidence
supports it, that expected benefit justifies cost/risk, and that less-invasive
options were considered. Record the informed-consent discussion (understanding of
diagnosis/prognosis, options, risks/benefits, alternatives, voluntary agreement),
especially for high-risk treatments (opioids, chemotherapy, surgery). A defensible
plan includes clinical rationale, risk-benefit analysis, patient warnings, and a
follow-up plan for abnormal findings.

## When to revise

**Scheduled:** chronic disease every 3-6 mo; behavioral health every 30-90 days by
acuity; rehabilitation weekly-biweekly during active therapy; annual comprehensive
update for all chronic conditions.

**Triggered:** significant change in status, new diagnosis, goals achieved or
stalled, patient preference change, hospitalization/ED visit, medication change or
adverse event. Document date, reason, what changed, and keep prior versions.

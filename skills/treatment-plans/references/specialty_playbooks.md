# Specialty Playbooks

Assessment tools, targets, and clinical pearls for the six plan types, plus worked
examples of concise-vs-verbose writing. Pick the playbook that matches the case.

## Writing concisely (applies to every specialty)

Cut filler ("the patient will…", "it is anticipated that…"), prefer bullets to
paragraphs, group related items, use standard abbreviations (define uncommon ones
once), and lead with what/when/why. Two illustrations:

- **Verbose:** a paragraph re-explaining the pathophysiology of T2DM and every
  microvascular/macrovascular complication.
  **Concise:** "Key education: disease understanding, micro/macrovascular risks,
  glucose/BP self-monitoring, med timing, diet basics, sick-day rules. Warnings:
  hypoglycemia (shaky/confused → 15 g carbs), severe hyperglycemia >300 (call
  office), chest pain/stroke symptoms (911)."
- **Verbose:** a paragraph narrating a weekly 50-minute CBT course.
  **Concise:** "CBT weekly ×16 (50 min): identify/challenge negative thoughts,
  behavioral activation, coping skills. Goals: PHQ-9 <10, return to work, 3 stress-
  management strategies."

## 1. General medical

**Use for:** chronic disease (diabetes, HTN, HF, COPD, asthma, CKD) and structured
follow-up of common conditions.

**Assess:** vitals, BMI, disease-specific metrics (HbA1c, BP, lipids, PFTs),
comorbidities, medication reconciliation, social determinants; stage severity with
validated systems (CKD 1-5, GOLD I-IV, NYHA I-IV).

**Targets (individualize):** HbA1c <7% for most (<8% if elderly/limited life
expectancy); BP <130/80 (<140/90 if elderly/low risk); LDL <70 mg/dL with ASCVD,
<100 moderate risk.

**Pearls:** deprescribe where possible (Beers); avoid NSAIDs in CKD/HF; caution
metformin if eGFR <30; cardioselective beta-blockers in COPD/asthma. Monitoring:
diabetes — HbA1c q3mo (q6mo if stable), annual eye/foot/urine-ACR/lipids;
hypertension — home BP averaging, BMP 1-2 weeks after ACE/ARB start; HF — daily
weights (report >2-3 lb/2 days gain), BNP when changing, titrate q2 weeks during
optimization.

## 2. Rehabilitation

**Settings:** inpatient rehab, outpatient PT/OT/SLP, home health, SNF.

**Validated tools:** FIM (18 items, /126), Barthel Index (/100), Berg Balance (/56;
<45 = high fall risk), 6-Minute Walk Test, Timed Up and Go (>12 s = high fall risk),
9-Hole Peg Test, goniometric ROM, Manual Muscle Testing (0-5).

**Goal levels (ICF):** impairment (ROM 90°→110°, MMT 3/5→4/5), activity (ambulate
300 ft with walker, transfer independently), participation (return to work, live
independently). Document assistance level: I, SV (supervision), CG (contact guard),
Min/Mod/Max A, Total A.

**Pearls:** modalities (heat/ice/TENS/e-stim) are adjuncts, not primary therapy.
Always include a home exercise program (illustrated, specific dosage, progression,
precautions) and DME/home modifications (walker type, grab bars, raised toilet seat,
remove scatter rugs). Discharge when goals met or plateau reached, patient/caregiver
educated, DME obtained, follow-up arranged.

## 3. Mental health

**Assess:** DSM-5 criteria; severity scales — PHQ-9 (depression, /27; ≥10
moderate-severe), GAD-7 (anxiety, /21; ≥10), MDQ (bipolar screen), PC-PTSD-5 → PCL-5,
AUDIT (alcohol, /40; ≥8 hazardous), WHODAS 2.0 (function); **suicide/violence risk**
via C-SSRS (ideation, plan, intent, protective and risk factors, access to means).

**Evidence-based psychotherapy:** depression — CBT, behavioral activation, IPT,
problem-solving therapy; anxiety — CBT with exposure (gold standard); PTSD —
prolonged exposure, cognitive processing therapy, EMDR; bipolar — family-focused,
interpersonal/social rhythm; borderline PD — DBT (1-year: individual + group + phone
coaching).

**Pharmacology:** SSRIs first-line (2-4 wk to initial response, 6-8 wk full);
antipsychotics — prefer second-generation, monitor metabolic syndrome/EPS/prolactin/
QTc; mood stabilizers — lithium (level 0.6-1.2 mEq/L, TSH, renal), valproate (levels,
LFTs, CBC), lamotrigine (titrate slowly — SJS risk).

**Safety plan (required in every plan):** warning signs, internal coping strategies,
social and professional contacts, means restriction (firearms, medication limits),
reasons for living. Crisis resources: 988 Suicide & Crisis Lifeline, Crisis Text Line
(text HOME to 741741), local mobile crisis, ED. Note 42 CFR Part 2 protections for
substance-use records.

## 4. Chronic disease management (multimorbidity)

**Common clusters:** cardiometabolic (diabetes + HTN + hyperlipidemia + obesity),
cardiopulmonary (HF + COPD), renal-cardiovascular (CKD + HTN + diabetes),
mental-physical (depression + chronic pain + chronic disease).

**Prioritize:** life-threatening issues first; then high-impact modifiable
conditions; favor synergistic drugs; weave in patient priorities. **Synergistic
meds:** SGLT2 inhibitors (diabetes + HF + CKD), ACE/ARB (HTN + diabetes + HF),
beta-blockers (HTN + HF + CAD), statins (lipids + ASCVD + diabetes), GLP-1 agonists
(diabetes + weight + CV benefit). Deprescribe low-benefit meds; simplify regimens.

**Coordinate:** PCP anchors; specialists co-manage; care coordinator, pharmacist,
dietitian, social worker as needed; shared EHR; medication reconciliation after
specialist visits; post-discharge follow-up within 7 days.

## 5. Perioperative

**Risk scores:** cardiac — Revised Cardiac Risk Index (0 pts <1% → ≥3 pts 5.4%);
pulmonary — ARISCAT; VTE — Caprini (guides prophylaxis intensity).

**Pre-op optimization:** diabetes — HbA1c <8% for elective (delay if >9%), hold
metformin 24-48 h, hold SGLT2i 3-4 days (DKA risk), reduce long-acting insulin
~20-25% day-of; HTN — continue most agents, hold ACE/ARB morning-of, continue
beta-blocker; anticoagulation — warfarin hold 5 days (bridge if high VTE risk), DOACs
hold 24-48 h, continue aspirin for most, hold clopidogrel 5-7 days if high bleeding
risk; optimize anemia (IV iron if time-limited).

**ERAS:** no prolonged fasting (clears 2 h before), carbohydrate loading, multimodal
opioid-sparing analgesia, goal-directed fluids, normothermia, antiemetic
prophylaxis, early mobilization and oral nutrition, early tube/drain removal, VTE
prophylaxis.

**Milestones:** POD 0 — out of bed 4-6 h, sips; POD 1 — ambulate hallway, regular
diet, remove Foley, oral analgesia; POD 2-3 — ambulate 150+ ft, adequate intake,
pain controlled orally → discharge when mobile, voiding, tolerating diet, stable, no
active complications, safe home plan.

## 6. Pain management

**Assess:** location/radiation, quality, intensity (0-10 NRS), temporal pattern,
aggravating/alleviating factors, functional impact (Brief Pain Inventory
interference items), prior treatments. Classify: nociceptive (somatic/visceral),
neuropathic (burning/shooting/numbness), nociplastic (central sensitization,
fibromyalgia), or mixed.

**Multimodal analgesia** (additive/synergistic, opioid-sparing): non-opioids →
adjuvants (gabapentinoids, SNRIs, TCAs) → topicals → interventional procedures →
physical therapies → psychological therapy (CBT-CP, mindfulness) → opioids last.
Neuropathic first-line: gabapentin, pregabalin, duloxetine, TCAs; topical lidocaine/
capsaicin; refractory → tramadol or opioids.

**Opioid safety (CDC):** before starting — non-opioid therapies inadequate,
functional goals set, risk assessment (ORT/SOAPP), informed consent, treatment
agreement, PDMP check, baseline UDS. During — start <50 MME/day short-acting,
reassess q1-3 mo for *functional* improvement, UDS q3-6 mo, PDMP each Rx or ≥q3mo,
co-prescribe naloxone, avoid concurrent benzodiazepines, reassess at 50 MME and avoid
>90 MME. Taper (10-25%/week-to-month) if goals unmet, adverse effects, aberrant
behavior, or patient request.

**Emphasize function over pain scores** ("walk 1 mile, return to work") and integrate
CBT-CP — psychological factors (depression, anxiety, catastrophizing) perpetuate pain.

## Cross-cutting for all plans

Cultural competence (beliefs, interpreter services, religious practices); health
literacy (teach-back, plain language, 5th-6th-grade materials, confirm the patient
can execute the plan); social determinants (screen food/housing/transport, connect to
resources, address cost via generics/assistance programs); advance care planning for
serious illness (goals of care, healthcare proxy, directives).

# Goal Setting for Treatment Plans

How to write goals that are measurable, achievable, patient-centered, and
time-bound. Well-formed goals are the spine of a good plan and the thing reviewers
scrutinize first.

## SMART criteria

- **Specific** — exactly what will change, for whom, in which aspect of health.
  "Reduce depressive symptoms measured by PHQ-9," not "feel better."
- **Measurable** — a metric or observable behavior with a baseline and target.
  Quantitative (HbA1c, LDL, BP, PHQ-9, ROM degrees, MMT grade, walk distance) or
  observable ("demonstrates correct insulin injection technique").
- **Achievable** — realistic given the patient's capacity, disease severity,
  treatment efficacy, resources, time, and motivation. Know expected effect sizes
  (e.g., metformin lowers HbA1c ~1-1.5%); set incremental sub-goals for large changes.
- **Relevant** — matters to the patient and is clinically meaningful; aligned with
  the diagnosis and the patient's stated priorities. Ask "what do you want to be able
  to do?" and connect clinical metrics to that.
- **Time-bound** — a target date or timeframe plus checkpoints. Short-term (days-3
  mo), intermediate (3-6 mo), long-term (6-12+ mo).

### Vague → SMART

| Vague | SMART |
| --- | --- |
| "Better blood pressure" | "Systolic <130 and diastolic <80 mmHg within 4 weeks on lisinopril 10 mg daily + sodium <2300 mg/day" |
| "Less pain" | "Pain ≤4/10 (NRS) and walking tolerance 10→30 min within 6 weeks via SNRI + NSAID + PT" |
| "Improve depression" | "PHQ-9 18→<10 within 8 weeks of starting an SSRI + weekly CBT" |
| "Get stronger" | "Right quadriceps 3/5→4/5 MMT within 4 weeks with PT 3x/week" |

## Goal hierarchies

**ICF framework** (rehabilitation/functional):

1. **Impairment** — body function/structure: "increase shoulder flexion 90°→140°."
2. **Activity** — task: "dress upper body independently."
3. **Participation** — life role: "return to work as a teacher."

**Medical outcome levels:** biological/clinical (labs, vitals), symptom (patient-
reported), functional (what the patient can do), and quality-of-life. Balance all
four — a plan of only lab targets misses what matters to the patient.

## Patient-centered outcomes

Include at least some goals in the patient's own terms. Patient-reported outcome
measures (PROMs) — SF-36/SF-12, PROMIS, disease-specific scales (e.g., Kansas City
Cardiomyopathy Questionnaire for HF) — and functional measures (ADLs, IADLs,
occupational/social functioning) capture this. Link a clinical goal to a
patient-meaningful one: "Reduce HbA1c 8.5%→<7% in 3 months" **and** "improve energy
to play with grandchildren without fatigue."

## Shared decision-making

1. Acknowledge choices exist ("we can prioritize aggressive HbA1c lowering vs.
   minimizing hypoglycemia — which matters more to you?").
2. Present options with pros/cons and numeric risk/benefit.
3. Clarify the patient's values.
4. Integrate preferences into the goals.
5. Agree together and document the process. Decision aids (option grids, "what
   matters to you" worksheets) help.

## Special populations

- **Older adults** — functional independence and treatment burden often outweigh
  disease-specific targets; consider life expectancy and time-to-benefit; fall
  prevention and deprescribing may be primary goals.
- **Pediatric** — developmentally appropriate, family-centered, growth/development
  and school functioning; plan transition to adult care.
- **Palliative/end-of-life** — comfort and valued activities primary; psychosocial
  and spiritual needs; caregiver support; dignity and autonomy.
- **Complex multimorbidity** — prioritize the most impactful 3-5 goals, coordinate
  across conditions, avoid conflicting treatments, minimize burden.

## Common pitfalls

1. Provider-centric goals — ask the patient early, use their language.
2. Too many goals — prioritize 3-5, build on success.
3. All-or-nothing ("cure," "perfection") — value incremental improvement.
4. Ignoring barriers (cost, transport, support) — surface and problem-solve them.
5. Static goals — reassess and modify on schedule.
6. Only clinical metrics — add functional/symptom/QoL goals.
7. No patient buy-in — use shared decision-making and motivational interviewing.

## Worked SMART goals by condition

- **Diabetes** — ST: "HbA1c 8.5%→<7.5% in 3 mo via metformin 1000 mg BID + carbs
  45-60 g/meal." LT: "Maintain HbA1c <7% ≥6 mo; daily 30-min walk."
- **Heart failure** — ST: "Euvolemia (no edema, weight stable ±2 lb) in 2 wk via
  furosemide optimization + sodium <2000 mg/day." LT: "NYHA II, no HF admissions,
  walk 1/4 mile without dyspnea in 3 mo."
- **Depression** — ST: "PHQ-9 18→<10 in 8 wk on escitalopram 10 mg + weekly CBT."
  LT: "Remission (PHQ-9 <5), full-time work, social re-engagement 2-3x/wk in 4 mo."
- **Post-stroke rehab** — ST: "Right arm 2/5→3+/5 MMT and FIM 85→100 in 4 wk with
  PT/OT 5x/wk." LT: "Independent ADLs, ambulate 500 ft with cane, discharge home in
  3 mo."
- **Chronic low back pain** — ST: "Pain 7/10→4/10 and walk tolerance 10→30 min in 6
  wk (SNRI + NSAID + PT)." LT: "Return to modified-duty work in 3 mo; 50% reduction
  in pain interference (BPI)."
- **Hypertension** — ST: "BP 152/94→<140/90 in 4 wk (lisinopril 10 mg + sodium
  <2300 mg/day)." LT: "Maintain <130/80; reduce 10-yr ASCVD risk 15%→<10%."

## Assessment tools worth naming

Goal Attainment Scaling (personalized), Canadian Occupational Performance Measure
(patient-identified functional goals), and PROMIS (standardized PROMs).

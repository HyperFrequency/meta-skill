# Patient Documentation (SOAP, H&P, Discharge, Consult)

Clinical notes record encounters, progress, and care plans. Good documentation
supports continuity of care, medical-legal protection, and billing (evaluation
and management, "E/M", codes). Write objectively; document medical necessity;
sign and date everything.

---

## SOAP notes (progress notes)

The default format for ongoing encounters.

- **S — Subjective**: the patient's reported symptoms/concerns, history of
  present illness, relevant review of systems, patient's own words. Example:
  *"Reports worsening exertional dyspnea over 3 days. Denies chest pain, fever,
  cough."*
- **O — Objective**: measurable findings — vital signs, exam by system, labs and
  imaging.

  ```
  Vitals: T 98.6F, BP 142/88, HR 92, RR 22, SpO2 91% RA
  General: mild respiratory distress
  CV: regular rhythm, no murmur
  Pulm: bibasilar crackles
  Ext: 2+ pitting edema bilaterally
  ```

- **A — Assessment**: clinical impression / diagnosis, differential, severity and
  stability, progress toward goals. Number the problems.

  ```
  1. Acute decompensated heart failure, NYHA III
  2. Hypertension, poorly controlled
  3. CKD stage 3
  ```

- **P — Plan**: diagnostics, therapeutics (drug/dose/route/frequency), education,
  and follow-up — ideally mapped problem-by-problem.

  ```
  Dx: BNP, CXR, echocardiogram
  Rx: furosemide 40 mg PO BID; continue lisinopril 10 mg daily; fluid restrict 1.5 L/day
  Edu: worsening-HF signs, daily weights
  F/U: cardiology in 1 week; call if weight gain >2 lb/day
  ```

Tips: concise but complete; standard abbreviations only (avoid the Do-Not-Use
list — see `regulatory_and_terminology.md`); document time of encounter; record
the patient's response to treatment.

## History and Physical (H&P)

The comprehensive assessment at admission or an initial encounter.

1. **Chief complaint (CC)** — one line, patient's words ("Chest pain for 2
   hours").
2. **History of present illness (HPI)** — chronological narrative. For pain, use
   **OPQRST**: Onset, Provocation/Palliation, Quality, Region/Radiation,
   Severity (0–10), Timing.
3. **Past medical history (PMH)** — chronic conditions, prior hospitalizations,
   surgeries (with years).
4. **Medications** — current drugs with dose/frequency, OTC, supplements.
5. **Allergies** — agent + reaction ("penicillin — rash; shellfish —
   anaphylaxis").
6. **Family history (FH)** — first-degree relatives' conditions; hereditary
   disease.
7. **Social history (SH)** — tobacco (pack-years), alcohol, substances,
   occupation, living situation, relevant sexual history.
8. **Review of systems (ROS)** — systematic, ~10–14 systems, pertinent positives
   and negatives (Constitutional, Eyes, ENT, CV, Resp, GI, GU, MSK, Skin, Neuro,
   Psych, Endocrine, Heme/Lymph, Allergic/Immunologic).
9. **Physical examination** — vitals, general appearance, then by system (HEENT,
   Neck, CV, Pulm, Abdomen, Ext, Neuro, Skin).
10. **Assessment and plan** — numbered problem list, each with assessment,
    diagnostic and therapeutic plan, and disposition.

## Discharge summaries

Communicate the hospital stay to the outpatient team. Aim to complete within
24–48 hours of discharge.

1. **Identification** — name/DOB/MRN, admission and discharge dates, attending.
2. **Reason for hospitalization** — presenting problem, chief complaint.
3. **Hospital course** — chronological/by-problem narrative: key findings,
   procedures, response to treatment, complications, consultations.
4. **Discharge diagnoses** — primary, secondary, complications, comorbidities.
5. **Procedures performed**.
6. **Discharge medications** — full reconciled list; flag changes from
   admission and new drugs with indications.
7. **Discharge condition** — stable/improved/unchanged/expired; functional and
   mental status.
8. **Disposition** — home, SNF, rehab, hospice; with/without services.
9. **Follow-up** — scheduled appointments, recommended timing, pending
   tests/results, referrals.
10. **Patient instructions** — activity, diet, wound care, medication
    instructions, and warning signs that warrant return.

Best practices: highlight pending results explicitly; document code-status
discussions; write for an outpatient reader who was not present.

## Consult notes

Answer the referring clinician's specific question. Include: reason for consult
and requesting provider; focused history and exam relevant to the question;
review of pertinent data; assessment; and clear recommendations (what to do,
what to monitor, what to follow up). Keep recommendations actionable and state
who owns each action.

---

## Documentation and billing

E/M code level is driven by medical decision-making (or, per current guidelines,
total time). Document enough to support the level billed: the problems addressed,
data reviewed, and risk. Do not upcode; do not copy-forward stale data
(a frequent audit and safety failure).

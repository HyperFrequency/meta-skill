# RLAIF vs RLHF

RLAIF (RL from AI Feedback) replaces the human preference labels of RLHF with
preferences generated by an LLM judging against the constitution.

## What changes

| Aspect            | RLHF                          | RLAIF (Constitutional AI)             |
|-------------------|-------------------------------|---------------------------------------|
| Preference source | Human annotators              | LLM evaluating against a constitution |
| Cost / latency    | High (human labeling)         | Inference cost only                   |
| Scalability       | Limited by annotator capacity | Scales with compute                   |
| Auditability      | Labels are opaque             | Critiques are explicit, inspectable   |
| Harm exposure     | Humans read harmful content   | No human reads harmful samples        |

## Performance notes

- Anthropic reported CAI/RLAIF models reach a comparable or better
  harmlessness/helpfulness Pareto frontier than RLHF on their evaluations, while
  being markedly less evasive (fewer flat refusals). See arXiv:2212.08073.
- The RL phase still uses standard RL machinery (PPO over a learned reward
  model); only the *source* of the preference data differs. Compute is therefore
  similar to RLHF-PPO and higher than offline methods like DPO.
- Preference quality is bounded by the judge model. A weak judge yields noisy
  rewards — ensemble multiple judges and majority-vote (see training-workflows.md).

## When to choose which

- **RLAIF / CAI**: scalable safety training, no human harm-label budget, want
  explainable preferences, want to avoid exposing annotators to harmful content.
- **RLHF**: need human-validated safety guarantees for high-stakes deployment, or
  the behavior you want is hard to express as written principles.
- **DPO / SimPO**: you already have a static human (or AI) preference dataset and
  want a cheaper offline objective without an explicit reward model + PPO loop.
